## Quick note on the public tables launch

We've started with heads-up, tournament style, ranked matches. The rating system we're using is Glicko-2, and we're
trying ot fine tune it to this style of play. More on that in a different blog post. The launch went well, no major
bugs, we're doing about 500-1000 matches a day on a small active user base - so it's a solid start. It is great for new
players to learn the game with some free poker action. The ratings keep the tables relatively honest and give a real
sense of progress as well.

You can try playing here - [Play a head's up match](https://app.chipsoffury.com/?tab=public)

## Something I didn't foresee, but was a pleasant surprise

I started playing a lot more often on Chips of Fury myself!

Initially I built chips of fury to scratch my own itch. It was a virtual chips app when it first launched in 2018. Since
then, it has evolved into a full-featured poker app. But somewhere along the way, I myself had stopped playing poker.
Developing the app took so much time, that playing recreationally became very rare. Also, life happened, my old poker
group is now in different cities. We all got married, had kids, so it's not an activity that fits in the regular
programming of life anymore :)

But since public tables have launched, I am able to put in a few games a day when I can find time. The format is short,
so you can play a game while waiting in a queue, waiting for your food to arrive at a restaurant, or on commute etc.
I've realized I'm not as good a poker player as I thought I was.

Anyway, the rather 'unexpected' surprise from all that is, I'm now using the app every day, and am a user myself again.
It feels like 2018 once more in that sense. I am now noticing so many small things that can improve the UX by a notch
here and there.
